I'm Building a Fintech From Scratch. Here's What I Don't Know Yet

An honest account of building SNOKPay from scratch — the frustration that started it, the questions I still have, and why I'm building anyway.

By Olukunle Stephen Gbolahan · September 23, 2026

I am building a fintech platform called SNOKPay.

I don't know if it will work.

That's an unusual way to start an article about a business. Most founders open with confidence — the vision, the market size, the problem they're solving for millions.

I'd rather start with the truth. Because the truth is more useful to anyone reading this.

What I actually know

I know what frustrates me.

Paying bills in Nigeria is harder than it should be. Buying electricity. Buying data. Paying for water. Dealing with estimated billing. Standing in a business centre while the person in front of you argues about a token that didn't arrive.

I know this because I've done it. Not because I read about it. Not because I saw a woman in a market once. Because I've stood in those queues myself.

That's my starting point. Not a market report. A frustration.

What I don't know

I don't know if people will use SNOKPay.

I don't know if the timing is right. I don't know if I can build it the way I'm imagining. I don't know if the margins work. I don't know if I'll find the right partners. I don't know if I'll run out of money first.

I don't know if my fashion brand, Stillnotok, will keep going long enough to fund this. Some months it barely holds.

I don't know how long it will take.

These are not things I'm hiding. They're the actual state of things.

Why I'm building anyway

I named my fashion brand Stillnotok because I refuse to pretend things are fine.

I'm naming my fintech SNOKPay because I refuse to only complain.

That's the whole philosophy. I can complain about how hard it is to pay a bill in Nigeria, or I can try to build something that makes it slightly easier. Complaining is free. Building is not. But only one of them changes anything.

I studied Human Resource Management at Lagos State University. Not computer science. Not engineering. Not finance.

So I'm building this as someone who has to learn as I go — who will get things wrong, who will have to ask for help, who will pay for mistakes. That is not a strong pitch. It is a true one.

What I think is actually broken

I don't have data to prove this. I only have my own experience and what I've heard from friends and family.

But here is what I believe is broken:

  • Paying bills is fragmented. Different platforms for electricity. Different ones for airtime. Different ones for data. Different receipts. No single place to see what you've paid.
  • Trust is low. People don't fully trust digital payment. There are too many stories of money sent and value not received. So some people still prefer to hand cash to a human being, even if it costs them time.
  • Record-keeping is a mess. Many small traders don't have a clear record of what they've paid. That can make budgeting and planning harder.
  • Convenience is uneven. My experience is that some people have easier access to digital payment tools than others, particularly where connectivity, familiarity or trust is limited.

These are personal observations, not claims based on a formal market study. Part of building SNOKPay is learning which of these assumptions are actually true.

What SNOKPay is meant to be

The idea is simple, at least on paper.

One app. Pay electricity, airtime, data, water and other bills. Keep a record. Get a receipt you can find later.

That's it. Nothing dramatic. Just a tool that removes a small everyday friction.

Whether I can build it, whether people will use it, whether it survives — that's what the next few years will tell.

Why I'm writing this publicly

Three reasons.

One: Writing forces me to think clearly. It's easy to carry a vague idea in your head. It's harder to write it down and see where the holes are.

Two: I want a record. In five years, I want to be able to look back and see what I thought, what I got wrong, and what actually happened. Most founders rewrite their history. I'd rather document mine as it happens.

Three: I want someone else to see it. Somebody else out there is trying to build something in Nigeria and feeling alone. I want them to see that it's not just them. That it's normal to not know. That you can start anyway.

What I'm not claiming

I'm not claiming SNOKPay will fix Nigeria's payment problems.

I'm not claiming I know the market better than people who've been in fintech for years.

I'm not claiming to be an expert. I'm not claiming certainty I don't have.

I'm just building. Slowly. In public.

The honest position

I'm a Human Resource Management graduate from LASU, from Oyo State, currently based in Lagos. I run a small fashion brand that has good months and bad months. I'm building a fintech product that isn't finished yet. I'm figuring out my life as I go, like most people my age.

I don't have millions in funding. I don't have a big team. I don't have a track record anyone would be impressed by.

I have an idea, a lot of frustration, and the stubbornness to keep going.

That's it. That's the whole thing.

A question

If you live in Nigeria, I want to know:

What is the most annoying part of paying a bill for you?

Not the biggest problem. The most annoying one. The one that makes you sigh every time.

I'm collecting answers. Not for research. For perspective.

Reply, or send me a DM. I read everything.

About the author
